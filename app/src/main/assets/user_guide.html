§ 02 / 13 Summon

Five ways to summon

because the bubble shouldn't always be in your face

You'll usually leave the bubble visible and just tap it. For times you don't, WiM ships five alternate ways to call it back without opening the app:

  1. Shake the phone twice. Quick wrist-flick × 2 (about half a second between flicks). The bubble appears at its last position. Same gesture hides it again. Off by default; turn on in Settings.
  2. Pull down the quick-settings shade and tap the WiM tile. The bubble drops onto whatever app you were just in.
  3. Add the home-screen widget (1 × 1 cell). One tap summons the bubble. Useful for muscle memory if you don't like the QS shade.
  4. Open the WiM app. The dashboard always re-summons the bubble on launch. Slowest path; mostly for fixing settings.
Note
The bubble survives reboots — if all permissions are still granted, it comes back on its own a few seconds after the phone finishes booting. You shouldn't need to summon it after a reboot.

§ 04 / 13 Pipeline

What happens to your voice

the whole chain, top to bottom

From the second you tap the bubble to the moment your sentence lands in the text field, your voice passes through this sequence. Knowing it makes the bubble's state badges legible.

01

Capture on the phone

Microphone picks up audio. For Layer I, nothing leaves the phone — the audio stays in RAM and the speech recognizer runs locally.

02

Speech-to-text layer-dependent

Layer I uses a small on-device Whisper model (tiny.en, English) — instant, offline, private. Layers II–III use cloud transcription and reconstruction. Layer IV uses cloud Whisper-1 because its richer confidence signals help with heavier speech disfluency.

03

Disfluency filter rule-based

No AI in this step. Plain pattern rules strip "p-p-pop" → "pop", "I I I want" → "I want" (but keeps "bye bye" and "had had"), and removes um, uh, like, you know, plus the well-known Whisper hallucinations ("thanks for watching" and friends).

04

Reconstruction layer II and above

Layer II/III: GPT-4o rewrites in your selected tone. Layer IV: Claude Sonnet 4.6 with extended thinking — reasons through the speech-disfluency pattern, builds a phoneme-difficulty map, detects anticipatory blocks, and reconstructs accordingly.

05

Safety filters always

Leak filter catches model responses that aren't real reconstruction (the occasional "I'm sorry, I can't"). Length guard: if the output is more than 2.5× the input length, the model embellished — WiM uses the cleaned raw text instead.

06

Inject accessibility service

The final text drops into the focused text field of whatever app you're in. If no text field is focused at that moment, WiM puts the result on the clipboard instead and tells you — better than silently throwing it away.

07

Learn layer III only

Your profile grows only from what you teach it — the corrections and vocabulary you add in Profile. WiM never guesses a correction from its own rewrites. Layer III and Layer I (which reads the same profile) both use what you added.

You"I I I want to to to call mom um about the the thing"
L1 out"I want to call mom about the thing"
L2 out"I want to call mom about the thing."
L3 out"I want to call mom about the order." ← your vocabulary swapped "thing" → "order"
L4 out"I want to call mom about the order." ← same, with deeper reconstruction when speech is heavier
§ 05 / 13 Layers

The four layers

pick higher when you want more help

You pick the layer once. WiM remembers it. You can change it whenever from the dashboard Layer tile or the bubble menu.

Layer I — Transcribe
on-device · ~1s · free · private

Local Whisper-tiny + the deterministic disfluency filter. No AI rewriting. No internet needed after the model has downloaded (about 40 MB, downloads once on first launch). Best for short, fluent dictation in apps where you don't need polish.

Layer II — Reconstruct
cloud · 2–4s · WiM Cloud plan

Adds cloud GPT-4o on top of L1. Smooths grammar, applies your tone (Casual or Professional), fixes punctuation properly. Doesn't know your personal vocabulary. Generic but very capable.

Layer III — Profile
cloud · 2–4s · WiM Cloud plan

Layer II plus your personal vocabulary mixed into the prompt. The longer you use WiM, the better Layer III gets at words that are specific to you — names, jargon, brand spellings, your way of saying things, as you add them in Profile.

Layer IV — Speech Disfluency
cloud · 6–12s · WiM Cloud Plus plan

Cloud Whisper-1 with detailed timing and confidence signals, plus deeper reconstruction. It considers phoneme difficulty, avoidance patterns, and anticipatory blocks. Slowest, but designed for speech that the lighter layers garble.

When to bump up
Start at Layer I and let it surprise you. Move up when you find yourself editing the output every time. Layer IV is purpose-built for harder speech and gets disproportionately better there — try it on the recordings that other layers garble.
Plans
Layer I is free. No account, no sign-in, nothing leaves the phone. Layers II and III need WiM Cloud ($4.99/month): 300 cloud takes and 150 minutes of audio a month. Layer IV needs WiM Cloud Plus ($7.99/month): everything in WiM Cloud plus up to 150 Layer IV takes a month. Sign in with Google in Settings, then tap Subscribe — Google Play handles the payment. Plans renew monthly until you cancel them in Google Play. Terms: mybakingcreations.com/wim-terms

Important: WiM is an assistive dictation tool, not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical condition. Consult a qualified healthcare professional for medical advice.

§ 06 / 13 Tone

Tone

work voice vs. real voice

Casual

Sentence fragments. Contractions stay. "yeah" stays. Lowercase casual. Default for messengers, social, personal texts.

Professional

Full sentences. Proper punctuation. No contractions in formal phrasing. Default for email, work chat, documents.

Auto-tone is on by default. WiM looks at the foreground app — Gmail, Slack, Outlook, Teams, LinkedIn, docs/sheets, work-style tools → Professional. WhatsApp, Messenger, Telegram, SMS, social → Casual.

Override globally in Settings → Tone. The override sticks until you change it. Tone only applies at Layer II and above — Layer I has no AI, so no tone.

Ping-pong guard
If you toggle tone via the bubble's bezel, that choice is respected for 30 seconds — even if the host app would normally auto-flip the other direction. Avoids the "I just set this!" race on app switches.
§ 07 / 13 Tap-to-send assist

Tap-to-send assist

tap the green bubble, message goes

When the reconstruction lands in a send-capable app, WiM doesn't send it by itself — that would be terrifying. Instead the bubble turns green for five seconds and gives you a one-tap "send for me" affordance.

  1. You speak. The text drops into the compose field.
  2. Bubble turns green, label reads SEND, the bezel arc counts down from 5.
  3. Tap the green bubble → WiM clicks the app's Send button on your behalf. Done.
  4. Or do nothing → after 5 seconds the green fades and the text stays in the compose field for you to edit/send manually.

Supported send-targets right now: Google Messages, Samsung Messages, Gmail, WhatsApp (and WhatsApp Business), Telegram, Signal — plus any app whose Send button is plainly labeled "Send". The list lives in Settings if you want to check or expand it.

Turn tap-to-send assist on or off in Settings → Tap-to-send assist. It is off by default. WiM never sends unless you first enable the feature and then separately tap the green bubble for that message.

Move bubble to Send button (optional)
Settings → Tap-to-send assist → Move bubble to Send button. Once on, the green bubble slides over the host app's actual Send button on SEND-ready, so the tap target is on the Send icon itself. Off by default; some find it unfamiliar, so this setting is off by default.
§ 08 / 13 Clarifier

The Clarifier

when WiM isn't sure, it asks

Sometimes the reconstruction lands a word the speech recognizer wasn't confident about. Instead of guessing, the bubble enters the clarifier — the bezel splits into a green left half and a red right half, and the flagged word in the pasted text is highlighted.

Tap left ✓Accept WiM's guess. Move on.
Tap right ✕Reject. WiM swaps in its second-best guess (or removes the word if it's likely a filler).
Press & holdDefer — leave it for now, you'll edit by hand later.
Voice: "yes"Approve hands-free.
Voice: "no"Reject hands-free.
Voice: "next"Defer hands-free.

The clarifier is bounded — never more than three flagged words per cycle. Most recordings get zero. The threshold is tuned to under-flag, not over-flag (false positives feel annoying; false negatives just look like a typo).

Tune sensitivity in Settings → Clarifier: Off · Low · Medium (default) · High. You can also blacklist apps from triggering the clarifier (code editors, password managers, anywhere prompts would be unwelcome).

§ 10 / 13 DAF

Delayed Auditory Feedback

a fluency aid, optional

DAF plays your own voice back through the earpiece with a 30–300 ms delay. It's a documented fluency technique — for some people, hearing themselves on a short delay reduces speech-disfluency severity in real time. Not for everyone. Worth trying if you haven't.

Turn it on in Settings → DAF. The slider sets the delay. Start around 100 ms; experiment by 20-ms increments. Headphones improve the effect dramatically; without them the earpiece works at low volume.

Not a cure
DAF helps some speakers and not others, and the effect can fade with familiarity. It's a tool, not a fix. If it helps, great. If it doesn't, turn it off and ignore this section.
§ 11 / 13 Privacy

Privacy

what stays on your phone, what doesn't

Local by default

Layer I Local keeps dictation audio and text on your phone. L1 Cloud and Layers II–IV upload a temporary WAV through WiM's authenticated backend for transcription. WiM deletes the temporary phone recording after processing and does not store raw audio in Firestore.

On the phone

Your settings, your personal vocabulary, your console log, your sessions database. Stored in private app storage. Only WiM can read it.

Cloud — only if signed in

Your sessions sync to Firebase Firestore so they survive a phone swap. About 1 KB per session — raw text, clean text, tone, layer, timing, success flags. No audio.

Cloud — for reconstruction

L1 Cloud and Layers II–IV send audio to OpenAI for transcription. Layers II/III send the transcript and selected context to OpenAI for reconstruction; Layer IV sends them to Anthropic. Those providers process the request under their API privacy terms. WiM does not use dictation content for advertising or sell it.

Toggle session backup off in Settings → Backup sessions. Delete your cloud account and data in Profile → Delete account and data. That permanently removes the Firebase account, cloud profile, synced sessions, and purchase binding. Local data remains until you clear app storage or uninstall. Deleting the account does not cancel a Google Play subscription; manage that separately in Google Play.

§ 12 / 13 Permissions

Permissions

five (sometimes six) gates

WiM asks for an unusual amount of access because the bubble has to live on top of every app and read/write text fields. Here's what each permission does and why WiM needs it.

01
Display over other apps

Lets the bubble float on top of any app. The bubble is the entire UI — without this permission there's no bubble, and WiM has nowhere to put itself.

Settings → Apps → Special access → Display over other apps → WiM
02
Accessibility

Lets WiM find the focused text field, read up to 200 characters immediately before the cursor for sentence continuity, insert your dictated text, and offer tap-to-send assistance. Password fields are blocked. Local Layer 1 keeps that context on your phone; cloud layers include it in the encrypted reconstruction request. Sending always requires a separate tap on the green bubble.

Settings → Accessibility → Installed services → WiM
03
Microphone

The bubble has to hear you. Recording only happens when you tap or hold.

Runtime prompt — tap allow when WiM asks
04
Battery optimization (exempt)

Android's battery optimization kills background apps to save power. The bubble runs in the background by design; without this exemption, Android will kill it whenever your phone idles.

Settings → Battery → Battery usage → WiM → Unrestricted
05
Notifications

Foreground services must show a low-importance notification. WiM's bubble notification stays in the shade so you can confirm the bubble is alive when it's hidden. No alerts, no sound, no badge.

Runtime prompt on Android 13+
06
Samsung deep-sleep exemption samsung only

Samsung adds a second, stricter battery killer on top of Android's. Without this exemption, the bubble dies overnight on Samsung devices.

Settings → Battery → Background usage limits → Deep sleeping apps → remove WiM
§ 13 / 13 Troubleshooting

Troubleshooting

when something looks wrong

The bubble disappeared
most likely a battery-optimization kill

Open WiM — the dashboard always re-summons the bubble. If it keeps disappearing on its own, check Settings → Battery → WiM → Unrestricted, and on Samsung devices also the deep-sleep list (§ 12 #06).

"Speech model not ready — try again in a moment"
on-device model still downloading

Layer I runs a small speech model on your phone. It downloads once, about 40 MB, on first launch — around 30 seconds on Wi-Fi. Until it lands, the free layer waits rather than guess. Layers II–IV don't need it; they transcribe in the cloud.

Text didn't paste — went to clipboard instead
no text field was focused

The bubble can only paste into the currently focused text field. If you started recording without tapping into a field, or the field disappeared while WiM was processing, the reconstruction goes to the system clipboard with a toast — long-press in any field to paste it manually.

Tap-to-send assist doesn't fire in app X
app isn't on the supported list yet

The assist needs to know the app's Send-button structure. Supported targets are in § 07. If WiM cannot identify the button, the reconstruction still lands in the compose field — you just tap Send yourself.

Recording sounds clipped at the start
tap-to-record latency vs. press-to-record

The bubble starts the mic the moment you tap. If you start speaking immediately, the first 100 ms can clip. Either pause a quarter-second after tap, or use press-and-hold mode — hold first, then speak, release when done. Hold-mode never clips.
